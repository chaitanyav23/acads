  1. Immediate Practice: Chapter 1 (Foundations)
  Matches Modules 1 & 2.
  Since you just finished these, do these specific problems to solidify the "Delay & Throughput" concepts which are
  high-yield for exams:
   * Packet vs. Circuit Switching: P8, P9
   * Delays (Transmission, Propagation, End-to-End): P10, and the example on pg. 37.
   * Throughput: P20 (Figure 1.20)
   * Layering: Review the "IP Stack" concepts.


  2. The "Kurose Gap" Warning: Modules 6, 7, 8, 9
  Matches Physical Layer Encoding.
  Crucial Note: The Kurose book (Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach) is famous for skipping the "Physical Layer"
  details like NRZ encoding, Manchester, PCM, or ASK/FSK.
   * What to do: Do not look for practice problems for Modules 6 & 7 in Kurose; you won't find them.
   * Alternative: Use Forouzan (Data Communications and Networking) or your lecture slides for these. Practice drawing
     NRZ-I, Manchester, and calculating PCM bit rates there.


  3. Next Step: Chapter 6 (Link Layer & Error Control)
  Matches Phase 2 (Modules 11, 12, 13, 16, 17).
  As soon as you start Modules 11-13 (CRC, Parity), jump back into Kurose Chapter 6:
   * Error Detection: P1, P2 (Parity), P5, P6 (CRC).
   * Multiple Access (Phase 3): P8, P9 (Slotted ALOHA/CSMA).


  4. The "Out of Order" Chapter: Chapter 3 (Reliable Transfer)
  Matches Phase 2 (Modules 14 & 15).
  Your study plan places Flow & Error Control (ARQ) in Phase 2. In Kurose, these are located in Chapter 3 (Transport
  Layer), not Chapter 6.
   * When you reach Modules 14/15: Do Kurose P27 (Sequence numbers), and study the rdt (Reliable Data Transfer) figures
     (rdt 2.2, 3.0) in Chapter 3.


  Summary Practice Path:
   1. NOW: Kurose Chapter 1 (Problems P8, P9, P10, P20).
   2. FOR ENCODING (6, 7, 8, 9): Use lecture slides/Forouzan (Kurose won't help here).
   3. AFTER MOD 12: Kurose Chapter 6 (CRC/Parity problems).
   4. AFTER MOD 14/15: Kurose Chapter 3 (Stop-and-Wait, GBN, SR problems).
